The whole talk in one sentence High accuracy is necessary but not sufficient in R2C2. What separated retrank from similarly accurate competitors was knowing when it had answered well.

The competition — what R2C2 is

NTCIR-19 R2C2 · "RAG Responses: Confident and Correct?"

  • Two subtasks, run in sequence. Phase 1 is passage retrieval (PR); phase 2 is answering with confidence (AC), where teams may answer using passages from any team's PR run.
  • Corpus. Movie articles from Wikipedia and Wookieepedia, pre-segmented into passages by the organisers.
  • Topics. 65 official questions. One was dropped from official scoring (an organiser-side judgment gap), so every official number is over 64. Our self-evaluated experiments score all 65.
  • Ground truth is LLM-generated. Official labels come from Qwen-3.5 and GPT-5.5, not human assessors. Every team used LLMs; the task presumes it.
  • The field. 7 teams, 25 AC runs: retrank, WaterlooClarke, ORG (organisers), slr2c2, Error404, BITEM, hit-u. 22 PR runs.

PR — passage retrieval

  1. Per question: up to 20 ranked passages.
  2. No length cap on passages.
  3. Scored after AC, by nDCG@20: a passage's relevance grade is how many relevant nuggets AC systems drew from it. Retrieval is graded by what answerers cited.
  4. We submitted retrank-PG-1…4 (24 Apr 2026, extended deadline).

AC — answering with confidence

  1. Per question: one answer, a confidence 0–100, and nuggets, each citing a passage from any team's PR run.
  2. Up to 4 runs per team. We submitted retrank-AC-1…4 (15 May 2026).
  3. Three official metrics: Accuracy, Mean Nugget Precision (MNP), HMR.

HMR — the metric that matters

  1. Harmonic mean of two rewards from Sakai.
  2. R_O: high when the system is not confidently wrong.
  3. R_U: high when the system is not needlessly diffident on right answers.
  4. Harmonic, so the smaller term dominates. The organisers call the target property modesty.

What we did — the paper in bullets

Thesis

AC is a calibration problem disguised as a QA problem. High accuracy is necessary but not sufficient; what separated retrank from similarly accurate competitors was knowing when it had answered well.

Pipeline — answer first, then verify (seven stages; the poster draws five boxes)

  • 0 · Pool. Top passages from selected teams' PR runs (submitted runs: BITEM-PG-1 + PG-2), deduplicated, re-ranked by a cross-encoder (ms-marco-MiniLM-L-12-v2), capped at 50.
  • 1 · Candidate answer. The generator reads the pool and returns an answer plus its self-confidence.
  • 1.5 · Answer-conditional refinement. Re-rank the pool conditioned on the candidate; keep 30.
  • 2 · Targeted nuggets. 3–8 atomic claims supporting the candidate, each citing one passage.
  • 3 · Bogus filter. Entailment-check each passage–nugget pair; drop unsupported ones. This drives MNP.
  • 4 · Independent verifier. A separate call re-derives the answer from the entailed nuggets only and compares: match_score ∈ {0, 1, 2}.
  • 5 · Confidence cap. No entailed nuggets or empty answer → refuse at 5. Otherwise cap self-confidence at 25 (score 0) or 60 (score 1); leave it unchanged at score 2.

Stages 3–5 are the calibration layer: a wrapper that can in principle sit on any answer engine. Confidence is anchored to the verifier, not the generator's self-report.

How it was tuned and run

  • val250: 248 held-out synthetic topics, sampled from ~15,000 we generated across 50 challenge types over 559 movies. Thresholds were chosen there; the 65 official topics were never used for tuning.
  • Models: Claude Sonnet 4.6 at every stage of the submitted runs. Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.7 only in tier ablations.
  • Four runs as diverse bets: AC-1 confidence saturation, AC-2 the clean main system, AC-3 accuracy-favouring, AC-4 a six-team-pool hedge.

Contributions — the paper's four

  1. A calibration-first AC pipeline whose confidence is anchored to an independent verifier rather than relying solely on the generator's self-report, and the finding that this, more than answer quality, is where HMR is largely won or lost.
  2. First place on all three official metrics, with the diagnostic that most of the field splits into two calibration failure modes (overconfident → R_O collapses; under-confident → R_U collapses).
  3. Method, not pool. We won on a competitor's passages while our own retrieval ranked last of 22 on PR. Phase-1 standing did not transfer to phase 2 across teams.
  4. A robustness analysis against LLM-based evaluation: cross-family self-evaluator validation (in-sample and held-out), a calibration-transfer experiment onto competitors' runs, a base-model swap onto three GPT models, and a no-LLM encoder-only lower bound.

Key results — the bullets to know cold

  • Standing: our four runs are the four highest HMR among 25 runs (0.868–0.974); next-best submitted run 0.761. First on Accuracy (0.969) and MNP (0.840). The MNP lead is significant (α = 0.05, randomised Tukey HSD) over every team but BITEM; HMR and Accuracy leads are point estimates.
  • Calibration: best run's mean confidence 0.956 vs accuracy 0.953.
  • The comparison: BITEM, accuracy 0.92, HMR 0.49; retrank, accuracy 0.95, HMR 0.96. Comparable answers, a 2× HMR gap.
  • Method not pool: our PR ranked last of 22 (nDCG@20 0.09–0.11 vs 0.44 top). PR vs AC standing ρ_s ≈ −0.94 across six teams; partly mechanical, so the claim is only that PR standing did not transfer. Adding weaker passages lowered HMR 0.963 → 0.868 (dilution).
  • Transfer: on other teams' fixed answers, our layer raised HMR for three of four non-quarantined runs; mean +0.065 (point estimate, no CI), under our self-evaluator. slr2c2 is quarantined.
  • GPT swap: the streamlined pipeline on three GPT models, under a fixed GPT judge: 3/3 positive, one significant, mean ΔHMR +0.27.
  • Judge check: self-evaluator matches official HMR to 0.0004 on our two main runs (in-sample); on five held-out competitor runs only weak (ρ = 0.60, not significant).
  • No-LLM floor: extractive committee, accuracy ≈ 0.52, confidence AUC ≈ 0.80. The LLM's measured contribution is answer accuracy at scale, not the confidence layer.

What did not work

  • Scaling up: all-Opus regressed HMR by 0.176, almost all in R_O (prompts held fixed at their Sonnet form, so this is naive substitution, not intrinsic capability).
  • Scaling down: with the Sonnet-developed prompts held fixed, all-Haiku regressed by 0.170 under self-evaluation: naive substitution, not intrinsic capability. Selective tiering (a strong model at the candidate stage) is what works.
  • Naive multi-team pooling: more evidence was dilution, not enrichment.
  • Per-topic oracle pool selection: verifier agreement ranks well but selects badly; it favours confidently wrong candidates on hard topics.
  • Variance-based uncertainty (MC-dropout, deep ensembles) added nothing in the no-LLM setting; the point estimate carries the signal.

Limitations — say them before you're asked

The paper's own caveats
  • All labels are LLM-generated; none of the numbers are human-validated.
  • The tightest self-evaluator agreement is in-sample; the held-out check is weak.
  • val250 is itself Claude-generated, so tuning generalises within a Claude-imagined distribution.
  • Transfer and GPT swap use surrogate judges; the official Qwen+GPT judge cannot be re-run.
  • n = 64 scored topics.
  • The coarse verifier cap (25 / 60 / unchanged, refuse at 5) is now the binding constraint: R_U is near saturation and almost all headroom is in R_O, in the upper tail the cap can't resolve.
  • The verifier shares the generator's model family, so their agreement is less independent than it looks.

Method: answer first, then verify

"Confidence is anchored to an independent verifier, not the generator's self-report. That is the key lever."

  • Pool other teams' PR passages → cross-encoder rerank → cap 50.
  • Candidate answer proposed early, then used to focus everything downstream.
  • Answer-conditional re-rank → targeted nuggets (3–8 atomic claims) → entailment filter that drops unsupported nuggets (this is what drives MNP).
  • Stage 4: a separate call re-derives the answer from the entailed nuggets only — no passages — and compares to the candidate.
  • Stage 5: that comparison caps the generator's self-confidence, or passes it through unchanged when the verifier fully agrees. Don't say "never uses the self-report"; the rule does use it.

Four things that didn't work

—
  • Scaling up, with prompts held fixed. All-Opus −0.176 HMR, almost entirely R_O (−0.27), not accuracy (−0.05). This is naive substitution, not a claim that Opus is worse.
  • Scaling down. All-Haiku −0.170. The useful configuration is selective tiering: strong model at the candidate-answer stage (−0.13 when downgraded), cheap downstream (verifier only −0.05).
  • Naive multi-team pooling. 0.963 → 0.868. Dilution, not enrichment.
  • Per-topic oracle pool selection. Also lowered HMR — verifier agreement is a good ranking signal but a biased selection criterion, favouring confidently-wrong candidates on hard topics.

What they'll actually ask

Isn't HMR gameable? Couldn't you just refuse everything?

In principle yes — an always-refuse strategy does score high HMR. But it does so at near-zero accuracy. We reach top HMR with the field's best accuracy: about 95% of topics answered correctly at high confidence. That's the distinction between being calibrated and being merely modest.

Your judge is Claude and you tuned against Claude. Isn't that circular?

The official labels come from Qwen-3.5 and GPT-5.5 — different families, so agreement isn't self-agreement. Our self-evaluator matches official HMR on our two main runs to 0.0004, but those are runs we tuned against, so that's corroborating, not held out.

The honest held-out number is weak: five competitor runs, ρ=0.60 on n=5, p≈0.35, MAE 0.12, with one large miss on slr2c2. We call it a diagnostic that orders the extremes, not demonstrated validity. It's in the Limitations.

You won because you had a stronger model.

With the Sonnet-developed prompts held fixed, naive substitution to Opus reduced self-evaluated HMR by 0.176. That's not a claim that Opus is worse; we didn't retune the prompts. What's informative is where the drop lands: R_O −0.27, accuracy only −0.05. Opus answers nearly as well, but under that verifier prompt its confidence stops separating right from wrong, and post-hoc recalibration doesn't recover it.

Subtracting the all-Haiku penalty from our main system leaves about 0.79 under our self-evaluator, against the best non-retrank run's 0.761. That's an estimate, not a submitted run, and I'm not claiming parity — that 0.03 gap is smaller than our judge's own held-out error. Only that a far cheaper model keeps the pipeline near the top of the field.

You used BITEM's passages. Isn't the result partly theirs?

The task explicitly permits pooling from any team's PR run, and yes — our winning runs ride on their retrieval, which we say prominently in both the paper and the poster. Our own PR ranked last of 22. The claim is specifically that the answering layer is what we contributed; the pool was a competitor's, and credit for it is theirs.

Is the ρ_s ≈ −0.94 PR/AC inversion a real finding?

Partly mechanical, and I'd rather say so than defend it. PR is re-scored by how often AC systems cited each passage, and our AC preferentially cited BITEM's passages, so the inversion is partly an artefact of citation choices. The defensible claim is weaker: phase-1 standing did not transfer to phase-2.

Why is the transfer experiment scored under your own judge?

Because the official Qwen+GPT judge can't be re-run on new outputs. Within the experiment the judge is fixed, so the paired delta isolates the confidence change, conditional on a fixed surrogate label set. But the absolute level is a surrogate number, and shared Claude-family agreement between our verifier and self-evaluator could still flatter the layer. That residual is stated in the paper.

Three confidence levels seems crude.

To be precise, it's three cap regimes, not three output values: refuse at 5, cap at 25 or 60, or pass the self-confidence through on full agreement. But yes, the verifier signal is coarse, and it's now the binding constraint. Because R_U is near saturation, almost all remaining headroom lies in R_O — which means separating near-boundary topics in the upper tail of the confidence distribution, exactly the region a three-valued verifier can't resolve. A finer-grained verifier score is the obvious next measurement.

Is the verifier really independent of the generator?

No, and that's in the Limitations. It re-derives the answer from a different view of the evidence — entailed nuggets only, no passages — but it's the same model family, so the two share failure modes and their agreement carries less information than its form suggests. Drawing verification from a different family would test whether the gain I'm attributing to separation is limited by their residual correlation.

n=64 is small. How much of this is noise?

A fair amount, and we grade accordingly rather than headlining weak results. One power analysis in the paper puts the minimum detectable accuracy difference at about 0.15. It applies only to the no-LLM committee comparison, not to the official HMR or Accuracy results. Findings are labelled robust, suggestive, or preliminary in the paper; the ablation CIs that cross zero are marked as crossing zero.

Did you try proper uncertainty estimation?

Yes, in the no-LLM arm, and every variance-based estimator failed: MC-dropout AUC 0.53, deep-ensemble score dispersion 0.51 — both near chance. What worked was the point estimate: the answerability margin reaches AUC 0.85. The useful signal is the estimate, not its spread. We'd predict a last-layer Laplace approximation behaves like the others since it targets the same posterior-predictive variance, but we didn't run it and say so.

Are your HMR and Accuracy wins statistically significant?

We didn't report a test for those. The paper reports randomised Tukey HSD only for MNP, where the lead is significant over every team except BITEM. Treat the HMR and Accuracy margins as point estimates. The HMR gap to the next run is large (0.974 against 0.761), but I'm not putting a p-value on it.

What's the uncertainty on the +0.065 transfer gain?

No interval is reported. It's a mean over four runs, three positive and one negative, under our surrogate evaluator. I'd describe it as consistent with the R_O asymmetry, not as an established effect size.

In the GPT swap, GPT-5.5 judges GPT answers. Isn't that the same family bias you said you avoided?

Yes, it can share family-level agreement, and the paper says so. The ON and OFF arms share answers and correctness labels, so the paired confidence delta is interpretable conditional on that judge, but it isn't judge-independent. It's also a streamlined pipeline, without the submitted reranker, 50-passage cap or entailment filter.

Is mean confidence matching mean accuracy just an aggregate coincidence?

It could be. A near-zero mean calibration error can hide poor per-bin reliability. That's why the paper includes a reliability diagram, and it's on a backup slide. Confidence tracks accuracy across bins, not just on average.

How held out is val250 if Claude generated it?

It keeps thresholds from being fit to the 65 official topics or their judge outputs. It doesn't escape the LLM loop: generalisation is shown within a Claude-generated synthetic distribution, not against human-written topics. That's in the Limitations.

Wouldn't more passages from more teams help?

Not naively. Adding weaker passages from a ten-run multi-team pool to the strong BITEM pool lowered HMR from 0.963 to 0.868. Pool quality mattered more than pool size.

Phrases to keep exactly as written

The paper is scrupulous about what it does and doesn't claim. Under questioning it's easy to round these up — don't.

  • "evidence for a design emphasis on calibration" — not "we beat everyone"
  • "stays in the competitive range of the field-leading team" — not "matches it on Haiku"
  • "phase-1 standing did not transfer to phase-2" — not "retrieval doesn't matter"
  • "under our self-evaluator" — attach this to every transfer number, every time
  • "direction is consistent, 3/3 positive, but underpowered" — not "the GPT swap confirms it"
  • "naive model substitution without re-tuning" — not "Opus is worse" or "scaling up doesn't help"
  • "anchored to the verifier, not the self-report" — not "never uses the self-report"
  • "almost all remaining headroom is in R_O" — not "all of it"
  • "under our evaluator it raised HMR for three of four" — not "it transfers"
