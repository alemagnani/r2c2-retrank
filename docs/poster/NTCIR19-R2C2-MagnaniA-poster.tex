\begin{block}{The thesis}
{\large
\HMR{} is \emph{not} a QA metric. It is a \textbf{calibration} metric --- the organisers'
notion of \emph{modesty}: be confident when right, diffident when wrong.

\vspace{0.6cm}
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}
\node[draw=rule,fill=white,line width=2pt,rounded corners,inner sep=14pt,text width=0.80\columnwidth] {
\centering\large
\textbf{BITEM}\; accuracy \textbf{0.92} \;$\to$\; \HMR{} \textbf{0.49}\\[0.25cm]
\textbf{retrank}\; accuracy \textbf{0.95} \;$\to$\; \HMR{} \textbf{0.96}\\[0.35cm]
\color{accent}\bfseries Comparable answers. Twice the \HMR.};
\end{tikzpicture}

\vspace{0.6cm}
The gap is \textbf{confidence discipline}, not answer quality.
}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Decompose \HMR: the bottleneck is $\RO$}
{\large
$\HMR = \mathrm{harmonic\ mean}(\RO,\RU)$ --- a harmonic mean, so \textbf{the
smaller term dominates}.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\RO$ --- penalises \textbf{over}confidence on wrong answers
  \item $\RU$ --- penalises \textbf{under}confidence on right ones
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.5cm}
Most of the field collapses on \emph{one} of the two:
}
\vspace{0.4cm}
\centering\normalsize
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Run & conf. & acc. & $\RO$ & $\RU$\\
\midrule

ORG \emph{overconfident} & 0.905 & 0.531 & \color{accent}\textbf{0.09} & 0.90\\
hit-u \emph{under-conf.} & 0.016 & 0.813 & 0.99 & \color{accent}\textbf{0.02}\\
\bottomrule

\begin{block}{``You just used a strong LLM''}
{\large
All R2C2 ground truth is LLM-generated, so we tested the objection rather than
asserting against it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item On our two main runs, self-evaluator matches \emph{official} \HMR{} to \textbf{0.0004} (in-sample);
        held out on 5 competitor runs it is only weak ($\rho{=}0.60$)
  \item \textbf{Base-model swap:} streamlined pipeline on three GPT models reproduces
        the calibration gain (\textbf{3/3} positive, 1 significant)
  \item \textbf{Cheapest tier} (all-Haiku) estimated at $\approx$0.79 (self-eval), in the competitive range of the
        next-best 0.761 --- not parity
  \item \textbf{No-LLM floor:} confidence is cheap (AUC 0.80); accuracy is not (0.52)
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.3cm}
Residual caveat: these use a surrogate judge, not the official Qwen$+$GPT ensemble.
}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Where the headroom is}
{\large
$\RU$ is \textbf{near saturation}; almost all remaining \HMR{} headroom lies in $\RO$ --- i.e.\ in separating
near-boundary topics in the \textbf{upper tail} of the confidence distribution.

\vspace{0.4cm}
Two limits we name:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Confidence is capped at \textbf{three levels} (5/25/60) --- too coarse to resolve that tail
  \item The verifier shares the generator's \textbf{model family}, so their agreement is
        less independent than it looks
\end{itemize}
}
\end{block}
